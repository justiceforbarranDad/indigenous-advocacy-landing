## SECTION 3: BACKGROUND - BARRAN'S STORY

### WHO IS BARRAN?

Barran was born in late March 2006 to an Indigenous family in Quebec. He has pre-existing medical conditions requiring ongoing specialist care: paraparesis (paralysis issues), biotinidase deficiency (genetic metabolic disorder), and DNA-related medical conditions.

Despite his disabilities, Barran was a bright, engaged teenager with dreams and potential. He attended school, received medical care from specialists at Montreal Shriners Hospital, and was supported by his family and healthcare providers.

### THE ATTACK: FEBRUARY 14, 2021

On Valentine's Day 2021, at just 14 years old, Barran was stabbed three times by a stranger wearing a balaclava at his mother's residence. The attack was violent and traumatic. He survived, but the physical and psychological injuries were severe.

**What makes this attack particularly tragic:** The family had filed 7+ reports with child protection authorities over seven years. None were taken seriously. The system failed to prevent what could have been prevented.

### THE AFTERMATH: SYSTEM ABANDONMENT

After the stabbing, the family desperately sought help:

**Medical Care:** Individual healthcare providers (Dr. D'Agostino, Christiane Côté, school consultants) showed genuine care and support. But government agencies failed to provide adequate mental health services.

**Mental Health Support:** Barran needed psychological help for PTSD, nightmares, and trauma. The DPJ denied requests for mental health support for 14+ months. This violated Jordan's Principle—the legal requirement to provide no-delay services to First Nations children.

**Financial Support:** The family needed emergency assistance for clothing, transportation, and medical costs. Government agencies offered sympathy but no action.

**Legal Support:** A custody battle for Barran's sister Paisley had been ongoing since September 2019. The legal system moved too slowly to provide emergency protection during the crisis.

### FIVE YEARS LATER: STILL FIGHTING

Now 20 years old (just turned 20 in March 2026), Barran continues to fight for justice and accountability. The system that failed him has not changed. Other Indigenous families continue to face the same abandonment, the same silence, the same institutional racism.

### ABOUT THE SYSTEM FAILURE

**Q: Could the attack have been prevented?**
A: Yes. The family filed 7+ reports with child protection authorities over seven years. None were taken seriously.

**Q: What is Jordan's Principle?**
A: A legal principle requiring no-delay services for First Nations children. The government failed to apply it in Barran's case.

**Q: What is DPJ?**
A: Direction de la protection de la jeunesse (Quebec child protection agency). They became involved AFTER the attack, not before.

**Q: Why didn't the family get mental health support?**
A: DPJ denied requests for 14+ months. This violated Jordan's Principle and constituted systemic denial of care.

### ABOUT THE GOVERNMENT RESPONSE

**Q: How many officials did the family contact?**
A: Over 500 at federal, provincial, and municipal levels.

**Q: How many responded substantively?**
A: Only 1.

**Q: What happened when they asked for help?**
A: They were mostly ghosted. When they persisted, they were threatened.

**Q: What was the cease-and-desist threat?**
A: MNA Céline Haytayan sent an official letter (November 19, 2023) threatening a criminal harassment complaint if the constituent continued asking for help.

**Q: Is that legal?**
A: No. It violates the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms s.2(b) - freedom of expression. It's also a chilling effect on democratic participation.

**Q: What is IVAC?**

A: They're demanding justification for 194 transactions, including ones as small as $5. This is harassment of an injured victim and family.

**Q: Is that legal?**
A: No. IVAC compensation is meant to help recovery, not be weaponized against victims.
