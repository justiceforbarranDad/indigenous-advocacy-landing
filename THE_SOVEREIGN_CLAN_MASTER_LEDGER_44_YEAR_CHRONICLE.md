## Phase 1: Recognition of Issues (Years 1-8)
During the first phase, the primary focus was on recognizing the patterns of negligence exhibited by state entities. This involved gathering testimonies, documentation of incidents, and initial outreach to affected communities.

## Phase 2: Mobilization and Advocacy (Years 9-16)
In the second phase, mobilization began in earnest. Advocacy groups formed, and community leaders rallied to raise awareness about the persistent challenges faced by those experiencing state negligence.

## Phase 3: Legal Interventions (Years 17-24)
With the momentum gained in the previous phases, this period saw significant legal challenges mounted against the state. Lawsuits were filed, and legal frameworks adapted to tackle the long-standing issues.

## Phase 4: Institutional Responses (Years 25-32)
During this phase, the state began to acknowledge the issues, leading to some institutional changes. However, responses were often inadequate and did not reflect genuine accountability.

## Phase 5: Ongoing Monitoring (Years 33-44)
The final phase covers ongoing monitoring of the promises made by state authorities. This section details how the community continues to hold the state accountable and the measures taken to ensure transparency.
