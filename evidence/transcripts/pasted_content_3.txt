URGENT COMMUNICATION: Five-Year Systemic Failure, Denial of Services, and Institutional Obstruction Against a First Nations Child Victim of Attempted Murder — Quebec, Canada

CHRC Reference: 100021349 | CHRC Inquiry: I2303084 | Website: justiceforbarran.org

Dear Special Rapporteurs,
I am James Robert McGovern, a Canadian citizen, and the father and legal guardian of Barran, a registered First Nations child of Dene heritage (Band: Hatchet Lake, Wollaston Lake, Saskatchewan), residing in Laval, Quebec. I write to formally request your urgent intervention in a case of systemic racial discrimination, denial of legally mandated services, violation of Jordan’s Principle, political intimidation of a victim’s family, and complete institutional obstruction that has continued unresolved for over five years against my children and our family.
We have exhausted every available domestic remedy at the municipal, provincial, and federal levels. Every institution — the DPJ, CDPDJ, CHRC, CISSS Laval, IVAC, 125 provincial Members of the National Assembly, federal Members of Parliament, the Senate of Canada (including Indigenous senators Senator Patrick Brazeau and Senator Michele Audette, former co-commissioner of the MMIWG National Inquiry), the Assembly of First Nations, and the Quebec Ethics Commissioner — has either refused to act, dismissed our complaints on procedural grounds, threatened us with criminal charges for asking questions, or redirected us to another institution that had already refused. We turn to your offices as our only remaining recourse.
I. THE INCIDENT — February 14, 2021
On Valentine’s Day 2021, my then-14-year-old son Barran was stabbed three times — in the left lung, in the chest, and in the back. An iPhone in his jacket pocket physically deflected two additional stab attempts that medical staff confirmed would likely have been fatal. He was hospitalized for six days at the Montreal Children’s Hospital, with a chest tube for three of those days. He was 5 feet 10 inches tall, weighed 300 pounds, and already lived with Type 2 diabetes and a documented mobility disability — conditions disproportionately prevalent among Indigenous youth in Canada due to intergenerational poverty and colonial healthcare deprivation.
The perpetrator of this attack was never convicted and remains at large to this day. Barran lives in ongoing fear. He is most comfortable in darkness; the attack occurred in daylight in the morning. Three people were at the scene of the attack. All three were denied recognition as victims and denied services. Barran’s first words leaving the hospital were: “I miss my siblings.”
II. SYSTEMATIC DENIAL OF SERVICES
A. Refusal of Psychological Care — April 2021
In April 2021, just two months after the stabbing, I formally requested psychological services for Barran from both the Direction de la protection de la jeunesse (DPJ) of Laval and from the Centre intégré de santé et de services sociaux (CISSS) of Laval. Both institutions refused. Not a single psychological session was provided to my son in the months following his near-fatal stabbing. The Protecteur du Citoyen (Quebec Ombudsman) subsequently confirmed in writing that the DPJ had failed in its legal duty of care. Despite this finding, no corrective action was taken and no services were ever provided.
B. Violation of Jordan’s Principle
Jordan’s Principle is a federal legal obligation of Canada, established specifically to ensure that First Nations children receive government services without delay, without denial, and without jurisdictional disputes being used as a barrier. At no point did the DPJ, CISSS Laval, or any other government body inform me or my family of our rights under Jordan’s Principle. We discovered this policy independently through our own research. When I subsequently requested that Barran’s file be transferred to the Montreal Native Friendship Centre — to access culturally appropriate services and a social worker familiar with Indigenous families — the DPJ refused that transfer.
C. DPJ Withdrawal Without Follow-Up — January 2023
In January 2023, the DPJ withdrew entirely from Barran’s file, with no transition plan, no referral to alternative services, and no follow-up of any kind. Following this withdrawal, Barran’s access to education collapsed. His mental health deteriorated severely. He is now 18 years old, receives approximately $300 per month in social assistance (the welfare minimum), and has been unable to work or attend school as a direct result of five years of institutional failure and untreated trauma.
D. Retroactive Harm from Amended Victim Compensation Legislation
The stabbing occurred shortly before Quebec amended its IVAC (Indemnisation des victimes d’actes criminels) victim compensation legislation. The amended law was applied in a manner that retroactively reduced the compensation available to Barran as a victim, despite the severity of his injuries and his pre-existing disabilities. Under the prior legislative regime, a victim with his documented profile would have received substantially greater support. This retroactive legislative harm compounded every other failure he had already experienced.
III. THE COMPLETE CIRCLE OF INSTITUTIONAL OBSTRUCTION
Over five years, we sought help from every institution available to us. Every one has refused, redirected, or dismissed. This closed circle is itself evidence of systemic failure:
•     DPJ / CISSS Laval (Provincial): Refused psychological services April 2021; refused cultural transfer; withdrew January 2023 with no follow-up. Protecteur du Citoyen confirmed failure in writing.
•     CDPDJ — Quebec Human Rights Commission (Provincial): CRARR filed systemic racism complaint April 2024. Dismissed 2025 using DPJ immunity clause under the Youth Protection Act. The commission ruled it could not prove “bad faith,” rendering systemic racism legally unchallengeable.
•     125 Members of Quebec National Assembly (Provincial): All 125 MNAs contacted repeatedly by email. Only 1 responded. The MNA for Laval-des-Rapides (Haytayan) sent a written communication threatening criminal harassment charges against our family for continuing to ask questions about DPJ conduct.
•     Quebec Ethics Commissioner / CED (Provincial): Confirmed in writing: no mechanism exists for citizens to file complaints against MNAs. All processes are confidential. No disclosure to complainants is provided.
•     Protecteur du Citoyen / Quebec Ombudsman (Provincial): Confirmed DPJ failure in writing. No enforceable corrective action resulted.
•     IVAC (Provincial): Retroactive legislative change reduced Barran’s victim compensation eligibility.
•     Canadian Human Rights Commission — CHRC (Federal): Filed complaint Reference 100021349, Inquiry I2303084. CHRC response: matter appears provincial; referred us to CDPDJ — which had already refused.
•     Federal MP Annie Koutrakis, Vimy (Federal): Jordan’s Principle is a federal obligation. Her office responded with a generic automated email. The federal obligation was not addressed.
•     Minister of Justice Sean Fraser (Federal): Contacted directly. No substantive response.
•     Senate of Canada (Federal): Contacted Senators Brazeau (Indigenous Senator), Audette (former MMIWG National Inquiry co-commissioner), Boisvenu, and Bellemare. No substantive assistance was provided by any senator.
•     Assembly of First Nations / AFN (National): Contacted directly. AFN stated it could not help.
•     APTN — Aboriginal Peoples Television Network (Media): Produced and broadcast a news report documenting Barran’s case. The report was subsequently removed. The family was given no explanation for the removal.

This pattern — in which every institution refers to another institution that has already refused — is the operational mechanism of systemic racism. No single actor needs to express racist intent. The architecture of the system guarantees that Indigenous families cannot obtain relief regardless of how persistently or how lawfully they seek it.
IV. POLITICAL INTIMIDATION AND SUPPRESSION
On or about November 2023, the office of MNA Céline Haytayan (Laval-des-Rapides) sent our family a written communication stating that any further contact regarding the DPJ or related government agency conduct “could be considered criminal harassment” and that police charges “could be filed.” This threat was issued in response to our family’s repeated, polite, and entirely lawful requests for information about why our Indigenous son was denied services following an attempt on his life.
The use of criminal harassment threats by an elected official to silence a victim’s family seeking government accountability violates freedom of expression, the right to petition government, and the right to an effective remedy — rights guaranteed under the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms and under international human rights law.
The APTN news report documenting Barran’s case was broadcast and then removed. No explanation was given to our family. The suppression of media coverage of Indigenous rights violations is a matter of serious concern that warrants attention.
V. APPLICABLE INTERNATIONAL LEGAL FRAMEWORK
•     UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (UNDRIP) — Articles 7, 21, 22, 24: The right to life, physical integrity, health, and social security of Indigenous persons; special measures for the protection of Indigenous children.
•     UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNCRC) — Article 23: The right of disabled children to special care and assistance. Article 24: The right to the highest attainable standard of health. Article 39: The right to recovery and reintegration for child victims of violence.
•     Jordan’s Principle (Canadian federal legal obligation): Non-discretionary duty to provide services to First Nations children without delay or jurisdictional dispute. Violated in its entirety in this case.
•     International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR) — Articles 2, 26: Non-discrimination and equal protection before the law.
•     Canadian Victims Bill of Rights (S.C. 2015, c. 13, s. 2): Rights of victims to information, protection, participation, and restitution — all denied.
•     TRC Calls to Action Nos. 1, 2, 5, 12: Specific obligations regarding Indigenous child welfare, access to services, and family support. Each remains unimplemented in this case.
•     CDPDJ’s Own 2025 Nunavik Systemic Inquiry: Confirmed the identical pattern of under-resourced, culturally inappropriate, and harmful interventions toward Indigenous children across Quebec.

VI. RELIEF REQUESTED
We respectfully request that your offices:
•     Issue an urgent communication to the Government of Canada requesting an immediate explanation of the failure to apply Jordan’s Principle in Barran’s case and the systemic barriers that caused this family to be denied services for over five years;
•     Call upon Canada to ensure that Barran receives immediate psychological, medical, and social services appropriate to the severity of his trauma, his documented disabilities, and his status as a First Nations victim of attempted murder;
•     Request that Canada investigate and report on the use of the DPJ immunity clause under the Youth Protection Act as a mechanism that shields systemic racial discrimination from human rights accountability;
•     Address the written use of criminal harassment threats by an elected official (MNA Haytayan) to silence a victim’s family, as a violation of the right to freedom of expression and the right to petition government;
•     Recommend that Canada conduct a formal audit of Jordan’s Principle implementation in Quebec, with specific focus on First Nations youth who are victims of serious crime;
•     Make public any communications with Canada arising from this case, so that the family may be informed of Canada’s response and hold it to account.

All supporting documentation — including written correspondence from government offices, the written threat from MNA Haytayan’s office, CHRC and CDPDJ complaint confirmations, the CRARR complaint, the APTN broadcast transcript, and Barran’s medical records — is available and will be provided upon request. Our public documentation is available at justiceforbarran.org.
We believe strongly that justice will come. We ask only that the United Nations bear witness to what has been done to our son and to our family, and that you use the authority entrusted to your offices to hold Canada accountable to the obligations it has made to the world.
Respectfully submitted,
James Robert McGovern
Father and Legal Guardian
Laval, Quebec, Canada — March 27, 2026
