The Commission was established under the Canadian Human Rights Act (the Act) to address allegations of discrimination by federally regulated employers and service providers. You can find an explanation of the Commission’s mandate on our website: https://www.chrc-ccdp.gc.ca/en.

In order for the Commission to accept a complaint:

The alleged discrimination must have been committed by an organization under federal jurisdiction, which include:
federal government departments, agencies and Crown corporations; banks; airlines; interprovincial transportation providers and shipping lines; telecommunications and telephone companies; television and radio stations; federally regulated unions; nuclear power operations; and uranium mines.
There must be a direct link between the alleged discriminatory act and one or more of the grounds of discrimination in the Act. The grounds of discrimination are as follows: race, national or ethnic origin, colour, religion, age, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, marital status, family status, disability, genetic characteristics and conviction for which a pardon has been granted.
We have carefully reviewed your correspondence, and it does not appear that the matters you raise are related to a federally regulated body. The respondent’s in your inquiry are all at the provincial level. As a result, the Commission is not the appropriate organization to deal with this matter. The situation you describe appears to be criminal in nature; this is not something that the Commission can deal with and should be dealt with through the legal justice system.  You may wish to seek legal advice and/or representation.

You may wish to bring your concerns to the attention of the (Québec) Commission des droits de la personne et des droits de la jeunesse.You will find their coordinates on our website at https://www.chrc-ccdp.gc.ca/en/complaints/provincial-territorial-human-rights-agencies.
