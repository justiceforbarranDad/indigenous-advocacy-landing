Whistleblower Report: of Allegations Against Sean Fraser for Misrepresentation to Chief Justice Richard Wagner and Systemic Failures in Upholding Human Rights for Victims of Violent Crime

## Executive Summary

This report serves as a detailed account of my whistleblower claims regarding systemic injustices in Canada's justice and governance systems. At its core is the allegation that Sean Fraser, in his capacity as Minister of Justice and Attorney General of Canada, has misrepresented facts or perjured himself before Chief Justice Richard Wagner of the Supreme Court of Canada. This is tied to broader failures in addressing human rights violations, particularly in the case of my 14-year-old child who was stabbed three times in 2021, resulting in severe physical, psychological, and educational harm with no adequate accountability or support from federal, provincial, or judicial authorities.

The report draws on personal experiences, public statements, and historical context, including the Truth and Reconciliation Commission's calls to action, which were given royal assent on June 3, 2021, in a ceremony involving Chief Justice Wagner. Despite Wagner's public emphasis on rights for the voiceless—especially vulnerable groups like Indigenous peoples, children, and victims of crime—the systems he oversees have failed to deliver justice in this case. This hypocrisy underscores a two-tiered justice system where leaders evade accountability while victims are silenced.

I demand investigations into these claims, resignations where warranted, and reforms to ensure human rights are upheld for all Canadians, regardless of status. Silence from leaders like Fraser, Prime Minister Mark Carney, and provincial officials in Quebec (including the CAQ government) makes them complicit in perpetuating tyranny over justice.

## Background: The Incident and Initial Failures

In 2021, my then-14-year-old child was the victim of a violent stabbing attack, sustaining three wounds that led to life-altering consequences. This occurred just months before significant legislative changes to victim compensation laws in Quebec, which retroactively disadvantaged my family. Under the previous regime, victims like my child—diagnosed at age 12 with severe disabilities including mobility issues and Type 2 diabetes—would have received support aligned with minimum wage until psychological recovery was achieved. Instead, the new law limited compensation to a mere $300 per month, insufficient for basic needs and far below welfare levels.

The attack disrupted my child's high school education entirely, leading to lost opportunities for a better future. As a father of six, I was forced to navigate a broken system where requests for aid were repeatedly denied. My spouse quit her job to provide full-time care, exacerbating our family's mental and financial struggles. Despite clear evidence of trauma, including medical reports and psychological evaluations, government bodies at all levels—federal (Justice Canada), provincial (Quebec's SJB_CAQ and DPJ child protection services), and local—refused intervention.

This refusal constitutes a violation of basic human rights under the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms, the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child, and international human rights standards. Victims, especially children, are entitled to protection, rehabilitation, and restitution. Yet, in this case, no one has been held accountable for the crime or the systemic neglect that followed. Appeals to lawyers, judges, and elected officials were met with silence or suppression, highlighting a culture of impunity.

## Allegations Against Sean Fraser: Misrepresentation and Perjury

Central to this whistleblower report is the claim that Sean Fraser has lied or perjured himself in communications or proceedings involving Chief Justice Richard Wagner. While specific details of the interaction remain opaque due to lack of transparency, Fraser's public statements and actions contradict the principles Wagner has championed, such as judicial independence and rights for the marginalized.

- **Context of Wagner's Role**: On June 3, 2021, Chief Justice Wagner presided over the royal assent ceremony for Bill C-5, affirming Canada's commitment to the Truth and Reconciliation Commission's 94 Calls to Action. Wagner emphasized that "we all have rights, even those with no voice to speak for justice," particularly highlighting vulnerable populations like children and Indigenous communities. This was a historic moment meant to address centuries of systemic abuse and ensure accountability.

- **Fraser's Alleged Deception**: As Justice Minister, Fraser is responsible for upholding these commitments. However, in handling cases like mine, his ministry has failed to enforce rights protections. I allege that Fraser misrepresented the state of victim support and judicial compliance to Wagner, possibly in private briefings, public forums, or through policy decisions that undermine Wagner's stated values. For instance, Fraser's refusal to address judicial vacancies and backlogs—issues Wagner has publicly decried—exacerbates delays in justice for victims. This is compounded by Fraser's defense of a "rigorous" judicial appointment process, which critics argue is partisan and politicized, as noted in public discourse.

Evidence from public records shows Fraser and Wagner appearing together at events, such as the Canadian Bar Association's annual meeting in February 2026, where both discussed threats to the rule of law. Fraser's remarks on not weaponizing the justice system ring hollow when victims like my child are denied recourse. If Fraser assured Wagner that systems are functioning equitably, this constitutes a lie, as our lived experience proves otherwise. Such perjury before the Chief Justice erodes public trust and violates ethical standards for public officials.

Furthermore, Fraser's portfolio mishandlings—spanning housing, infrastructure, and now justice—demonstrate a pattern of incompetence or deliberate neglect. Calls for his resignation are warranted, as his actions insult every Canadian victim seeking justice.

## Systemic Human Rights Violations and Lack of Accountability

This case exemplifies broader atrocities in Canada's justice system:

- **Two-Tiered Justice**: Leaders like Fraser, former Prime Minister Justin Trudeau, and Quebec Premier François Legault (CAQ) operate above the law, while victims are marginalized. The Truth and Reconciliation framework, signed under Wagner's watch, promised change, yet history repeats: Indigenous and non-Indigenous children alike suffer without voice or defense.

- **Silencing of Victims and Whistleblowers**: Attempts to raise awareness—through media, social platforms, and direct appeals to the Governor General—result in wellness checks or police visits rather than action. This intimidation tactic suppresses dissent and protects the elite.

- **Impact on Families**: My family has endured five years of trauma without support. My child's disabilities were exacerbated, education lost, and future dimmed. This is not isolated; thousands of victims face similar barriers, as evidenced by judicial backlogs nearing 80,000 cases.

- **Complicity of Institutions**: Organizations like the NHL, Hockey Canada, and media outlets (CBC, CTV) promote reconciliation symbolically (e.g., orange shirts on Truth and Reconciliation Day) but ignore real injustices. Their silence makes them accomplices.

International intervention may be necessary, as domestic mechanisms fail. Appeals to the UN Human Rights Council, Amnesty International, and global leaders are underway to expose this tyranny.
