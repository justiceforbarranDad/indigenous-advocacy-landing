## FACTUAL BACKGROUND

### Orange Shirt Day: A Sacred Memorial

Orange Shirt Day (September 30) commemorates the residential school system's cultural genocide against Indigenous peoples. It honors the memory of Phyllis Cormier, whose orange shirt was taken from her at residential school, symbolizing the theft of Indigenous identity and dignity.

### The Hypocrisy: Performative Activism vs. Systemic Abandonment

While your organization profits from Orange Shirt Day merchandise, **Indigenous children continue to suffer systemic abandonment:**

- **Barran's Case (February 14, 2021):** A 14-year-old Indigenous boy was stabbed three times. His family sought help from the DPJ (Direction de la protection de la jeunesse), which withdrew services in January 2023 with no adequate follow-up.

- **Jordan's Principle Ignored:** Federal policy guarantees no-delay services for First Nations children. This policy was systematically ignored in Barran's case and countless others.

- **Political Ghosting:** 50+ government officials at federal, provincial, and municipal levels have been contacted. Response: silence.

- **Institutional Failure:** CDPDJ (Quebec Human Rights Commission) rejected investigation requests. Protecteur du citoyen offered limited response. Legal Aid Quebec refused assistance.

## LEGAL VIOLATIONS

Your organization is in violation of the following:

### 1. **Canadian Human Rights Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. H-6)**
   - Section 2: Recognizes the fundamental right to equality and dignity
   - Your exploitation of Indigenous suffering while ignoring systemic harm violates this principle

### 2. **Quebec Charter of Human Rights and Freedoms (R.S.Q., c. C-12)**
   - Article 1: Right to life, liberty, and security of the person
   - Article 39: Right to equal protection and equal benefit of the law
   - Your performative activism while Indigenous children suffer violates these rights

### 3. **Unfair and Deceptive Marketing Practices**
   - Consumer Protection Act (R.S.Q., c. P-40.1): Prohibits misleading advertising
   - Selling Orange Shirt Day merchandise without donating proceeds to Indigenous child welfare organizations is deceptive marketing
   - Consumers are misled into believing their purchase supports Indigenous causes

### 4. **Intellectual Property and Trademark Violations**
   - Orange Shirt Day is a sacred Indigenous memorial, not a commercial brand
   - Using this memorial for profit without Indigenous community consent and benefit-sharing violates Indigenous intellectual property rights

### 5. **Breach of Fiduciary Duty to Indigenous Communities**
   - Organizations claiming to support Indigenous causes have a fiduciary duty to act in the best interests of Indigenous peoples
   - Profiting from Orange Shirt Day while ignoring systemic harm to Indigenous children breaches this duty

### 6. **Truth and Reconciliation Commission Calls to Action**
   - Call to Action #65: Establish a national inquiry into missing and murdered Indigenous women and girls
   - Call to Action #62: Implement Jordan's Principle
   - Your organization's failure to support Indigenous families in crisis violates the spirit and letter of these Calls to Action

## LEGAL FRAMEWORK FOR THIS DEMAND

### Protected Expression
This demand is made under **Charter of Rights and Freedoms, Section 2(b)** — freedom of expression and responsible communication on matters of public interest.

The exploitation of Indigenous children's suffering for corporate profit is a matter of profound public interest. This demand is made in good faith to protect vulnerable Indigenous communities.

### Precedent
- **Grant v. Torstar Corp., 2009 SCC 61:** Establishes the "responsible communication on matters of public interest" defense for statements made in good faith
- **R. v. Keegstra, [1990] 3 S.C.R. 697:** Affirms that hate speech and exploitation of vulnerable groups violates fundamental rights
