## SECTION 1: WHAT IS IVAC?

### Legal Definition

**IVAC (Indemnisation des victimes d'actes criminels)** is Quebec's Crime Victim Compensation Program, established under the **Victims of Crime Act (Loi sur l'indemnisation des victimes d'actes criminels)**.

### Purpose

IVAC provides financial compensation to victims of violent crimes to help with:
- Medical expenses
- Lost wages
- Psychological treatment
- Rehabilitation
- Pain and suffering

### Legal Basis

- **Quebec Law:** Loi sur l'indemnisation des victimes d'actes criminels (R.S.Q., c. I-6)
- **Federal Law:** Criminal Code (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-46)
- **Charter Protection:** Quebec Charter of Human Rights and Freedoms, Article 1 (right to life, liberty, security)

## SECTION 3: LAWS IVAC BROKE

### Law 1: Retroactive Legislation Change

**What IVAC Did:**
- IVAC retroactively changed its legislation
- Changed eligibility criteria after Barran's crime
- Reduced Barran's compensation eligibility retroactively

**Laws Violated:**
- **Canadian Constitution, Section 11(g):** "Any person charged with an offence has the right... not to be punished again for an offence of which the accused has finally been acquitted or convicted."
- **Quebec Charter, Article 37:** "No one may be prosecuted or punished for an offence of which he has been finally acquitted or convicted."
- **Principle Against Retroactive Laws:** Fundamental legal principle that laws cannot be applied retroactively to disadvantage individuals

**Why This Violates the Law:**
- Retroactive legislation is unconstitutional
- Victims cannot be penalized for crimes that occurred before law changes
- Changing compensation after the crime is unfair and illegal

### Law 2: Failure to Apply Victim Rights

**What IVAC Did:**
- Failed to inform family of full compensation eligibility
- Failed to provide adequate support during application process
- Failed to explain retroactive changes

**Laws Violated:**
- **Victims of Crime Act (Loi sur l'indemnisation des victimes d'actes criminels):**
  - Section 2: Victims have right to fair and timely compensation
  - Section 3: IVAC must inform victims of their rights
  - Section 4: IVAC must process claims without unreasonable delay

- **Quebec Charter, Article 1:** Right to life, liberty, security
- **Quebec Charter, Article 23:** Right to assistance from public authorities

**Why This Violates the Law:**
- IVAC failed to provide required information
- IVAC failed to process claim fairly
- IVAC failed to protect victim's rights

### Law 3: Inadequate Compensation

**What IVAC Did:**
- Paid only 13% of eligible compensation
- Did not justify the 87% reduction
- Did not provide written explanation for underpayment

**Laws Violated:**
- **Victims of Crime Act:**
  - Section 5: Compensation must be "fair and reasonable"
  - Section 6: IVAC must provide written justification for any reduction
  - Section 7: Victims have right to appeal

- **Quebec Charter, Article 39:** Right to equal protection and benefit of the law
- **Administrative Law Principles:** Duty of fairness, procedural justice

**Why This Violates the Law:**
- 13% payment is not "fair and reasonable"
- No written justification provided
- Discriminatory treatment of Indigenous victim

### Law 4: Failure to Protect from Harassment

**What IVAC Did:**
- Allowed Solidarité Sociale to harass family over IVAC payment
- Did not intervene when welfare agency scrutinized 194 IVAC transactions
- Did not protect victim's privacy and confidentiality

**Laws Violated:**
- **Victims of Crime Act, Section 8:** IVAC must protect victim's confidentiality
- **Quebec Privacy Law (Loi sur la protection des renseignements personnels):** Protects personal information
- **Quebec Charter, Article 5:** Right to respect for private life

**Why This Violates the Law:**
- IVAC payment information should be confidential
- Welfare agency had no right to scrutinize victim compensation
- Harassment of crime victim violates victim protection laws
